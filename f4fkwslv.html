01 / THE AIRCRAFT

MCDONNELL DOUGLAS · GERMANY

A classic airframe.
A new air picture.

The F-4F was Germany’s dedicated export version of the Phantom II: a two-seat, twin-engine interceptor adapted for Luftwaffe service.

As air-defence needs changed, a portion of the fleet received the KWS-LV modernisation. KWS means Kampfwertsteigerung—capability enhancement—and LV means Luftverteidigung, or air defence. The upgrade extended the aircraft’s usefulness without replacing its basic airframe.

The KWS-LV is associated with the F-4F ICE programme. It should not be confused with the separate KWS-LA ground-attack upgrade path.

WHAT THE UPGRADE CHANGED ↘

02 / KWS-LV · ICE

Old silhouette.
Newer systems.

A targeted modernisation centred on air-defence capability, cockpit updates, and compatibility with a newer generation of guided weapons.

01 / RADAR

AN/APG-65 FAMILY

The ICE upgrade brought an APG-65-family radar to the F-4F. A modern pulse-Doppler set gave the crew a more capable basis for intercept and missile integration.

02 / WEAPON INTEGRATION

AMRAAM COMPATIBILITY

New avionics and mission-computer changes enabled the air-defence variant to use the AIM-120 AMRAAM alongside its short-range Sidewinder fit.

03 / CREW INTERFACE

UPDATED COCKPIT

Displays and onboard computing were revised to support the new radar and weapons. The second crew member continued to share the workload from the rear cockpit.

!

03 / ARMAMENT

Interceptor tools.
Three key systems.

The KWS-LV’s defining change was beyond-visual-range missile compatibility. Its established cannon and short-range missile armament remained part of the air-defence picture.

04 / SERVICE CONTEXT

A bridge to
the next era.

The ICE standard helped keep the Phantom relevant as Germany moved toward the Eurofighter Typhoon. The upgrade concentrated on sensors, avionics, and air-to-air weapons—not a new airframe.

The F-4F served the Luftwaffe for decades, with Germany’s final Phantom flights taking place in 2013. Its angular shape and twin-engine roar remain a defining chapter in post-war German aviation.
